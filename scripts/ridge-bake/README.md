# ridge-bake

Offline baker for the ridge backdrop: it turns a Copernicus DEM into K+1 G-buffer cards per place, split by K authored seams and cut into 32 azimuth tiles round the whole 360 degree ring, plus a `manifest.json` (schema 3) and an `index.json` that the runtime reads. Each card tile is a colour WebP (albedo and coverage) and a geometry WebP (normal and log-distance). The baker is not shipped; `public/ridge/` holds its output. The client owns lighting, haze and ink: no sun, haze or shadow is baked.

## Run

```sh
python3 -I scripts/ridge-bake/export.py <place-id> --tiles DIR --out public/ridge/ [--work DIR] [--sheet contact.png]
```

Needs Python 3 with `numpy`, `tifffile` and `imagecodecs`. `--tiles` is a directory of Copernicus GLO-30 tiles (never committed); `--work` caches the mosaic and rays (default `$TMPDIR/ridge-bake-work`); `--sheet` writes the contact sheet at 1/4 of the ring's width (the ring composite, every card's strip alone and the depth image with every seam, the 32 tile boundaries drawn, lit by one fixed light). The bake prints `r_max_m`, the resident run and the worst fit error per card. `python3 -I scripts/ridge-bake/export.py --write-schema` rewrites `ridge-bake.schema.json`, which the tests pin.

Tests: `RIDGE_PY=<python> RIDGE_TILES=DIR scripts/ridge-bake/tests/run.sh [test_ridge.py|mutate.py]` (`mutate.py` re-introduces each rule's failure into a copy of the baker and fails if a test does not notice).

## Cards

`places.json` gives each preset `card_split_km`, an ordered list of 1 to 5 seam depths in km, and optionally `prefilter_m`. A preset without `card_split_km` is refused, and so is a split that leaves a card empty (the message names the splits); K splits make K+1 cards, at most `MAX_CARDS` = 6 per place. New places get a seam probe look before their first bake.

1. **March.** `stage0` marches 6400 azimuth columns of rays over the DEM with Earth curvature and refraction (`ridge.elev`), cached by a key of its inputs.
2. **Low-pass.** `prefilter` blurs the sampled heights with a Gaussian of `prefilter_m` metres along each ray and across rays at equal distance (sigma = `prefilter_m` / (distance x column width) columns), then recomputes the angles. Roofs go, hills stay.
3. **View.** `set_view` maps angle to row linearly: the highest skyline angle of the whole ring sits at 4% of the frame height, the lowest at 66%, and the horizon keeps `GROUND_ROWS_MIN` = 150 rows under it (the runtime's `groundRowsNeeded`: (1 - 0.85) x 600 / 0.6, so the plane reaches the bottom of every viewport at rest). The scale is the lesser of the two fits, clamped to 0.5x to 10x vertical exaggeration (rulings `ridge-ring-vertical-anchors`, `ridge-ground-rows-below-horizon`: every azimuth sits inside one frame; a place whose whole-ring relief leaves too few ground rows is flattened below 1x); a skyline that cannot hold the rows even at 0.5x is refused naming `sky_deg` and the floor. `export.validate_manifest` refuses a plane with fewer than 150 rows under the horizon and `manifest.ts` refuses it at load.
4. **Seams.** `coverage` counts the visible rows of the opaque view per log-distance bin and column. `seams` finds one seam per authored split per column by dynamic programming (seam carving, forward energy): it sits in a gap between depths, moving it costs 2 per bin plus the visible rows it sweeps (so it never hops across a ridge between two columns and splits it side by side), a seam inside visible terrain is cheap to cross-fade, the authored depth only breaks ties. Seam k stays 0.4 nat beyond seam k-1. The DP runs over the ring three times side by side and keeps the middle lap, so a seam is continuous across the wrap (column 6399 to 0) and a tile boundary is only a crop.
5. **Cards.** `cards` runs per tile, on that tile's columns and the matching normals (it is column-independent: a tile is a crop of per-column results, so no seam appears at a tile boundary). It makes card i from the first visible hit at or beyond seam i-1 (layered depth: a farther card keeps the terrain a nearer ridge hides), alpha 1 minus a smoothstep over seam i in log-distance (the cross-fade), crest rows with fractional coverage, distance and normal interpolated between samples by angle, albedo from a small height and slope material, cropped to the rows where alpha is not zero. The rows of card i > 0 below the angle of its first sample (hit and first sample of the card) are its **wall**: the extruded face of the ridge the card starts at, which keeps that sample's distance, wears the ground material at its height with the face up, and is flagged `wall`. Card 0 also draws the **near march** (ruling `ridge-near-ground-outline`): the place's own DEM from `NEAR_START_M` = 1 m to the near clip `NEAR_CLIP_M` = 500 m (`near_march`: float64 bilinear samples every max(1 m, 0.02 d) up to 10 m, about 170 per column, low-passed with the place's own `prefilter_m`), joined to the far march by `join_near` on the whole ring before the tile cut, its angles capped per column at the angle of the clip sample so the near ground draws inside the silhouette of the terrain at the clip (a near hill above that edge is cut along it). Its rows are ordinary terrain with their own normal and the albedo of their own height and n_up, flagged `near` in memory; `cards` refuses a march that starts at the clip. The runtime reads them as ground at the clip distance (they decode at `log_d_min`) and lights them like any card pixel. The alpha-over composite of the cards equals the opaque view; `check_cover` refuses a tile that differs by more than 0.02.
6. **Tiles.** The ring is absolute compass, column 0 = north, column c at azimuth (c + 0.5) / 6400 x 360; `places.json`'s `az_deg` / `look_at` is only the rest azimuth (`view.look_az_deg`). Tile t owns columns [200 t, 200 t + 200) (11.25 degrees) and its files hold [200 t - 2, 200 t + 202): two columns of real neighbour data each side (the client's ink pass and lighting read one texel each way; the client crops it), taken periodically by `ridge.ring_cols`. Per card and tile the file is cropped to the rows where that tile's 8-bit alpha is not zero (`top`, `h`); a card that holds nothing in a tile is `null` there (the client interpolates its distance from the neighbours); a card with no solid pixel in any tile is refused.
7. **Distance and the offset range.** The bake holds no camera (ruling `ridge-camera-full-ring`). `pack` gives each card tile `distance_m` = exp(median ln d) over the tile's solid pixels that are neither wall nor near ground (its interior 200 columns); the client derives each card's slide from that and the viewer's offset r and heading. `camera.r_max_m` is the largest |r| (never above half the nearest terrain's distance) at which, for every tile, card and phi (24 steps of the camera's angle on the circle against its heading; the offset-only rotation mode moves phi round the whole circle), the least-squares line `a + s beta` fitted to the exact view angle `atan2(R sin beta - r sin phi, R cos beta - r cos phi)` over the widest viewport (`HFOV_MAX_DEG` = 72 / 1.1) errs by at most `FIT_PX_MAX` = 24 card px (the continuous error: the line by quadrature over the viewport, the worst error looked for on 513 points, so a handful of samples cannot undercount it); the distance d is that of the NEAREST solid (alpha >= 0.5, neither wall nor near ground) pixel the card draws in the tile's interior columns, which is where the slide errs most (the line is fitted for the tile's `distance_m`, the median, but the bound is held for the nearest terrain: a tile whose terrain spans 600 to 2000 m is bound at 600 m); a tile whose card 0 draws only near ground (under the clip) has no such pixel and binds nothing; and the resident gate (below) must hold at r_max_m too. `camera.r_max_forward_m` is the same search at phi = 0 alone (same `FIT_PX_MAX`, the same half-distance cap, the same resident gate): the camera pushed forward along its heading, which is where the offset stays when the view direction turns with it (the modes 'still' and 'both'), so it is at least `r_max_m`; the client takes `r_max_m` for the offset-only mode and for a backward offset, `r_max_forward_m` for a forward one in the other modes (`view.ts` `offsetRange`). `FIT_PX_MAX` is provisional, tuned by eye (ruling `ridge-offset-range-fit`). A bake whose `r_max_m` is below `R_MAX_FLOOR_M` = 25 m is refused, naming the card and tile. `distance_m` and the nearest-terrain table exclude the near ground (a tile with nothing outside it gets `distance_m` = 500 m), so card 0's distances are never nearer than the near clip (`write_bake` refuses one that is), and `r_max_m` and `r_max_forward_m` hold for the nearest solid terrain each card draws in each tile, at or beyond 500 m, near-only tiles excluded: the ground nearer than that rides card 0's slide and is not measured.
8. **Normals.** `normals` takes the along-ray slope and the across-ray slope at equal distance (neighbouring columns, same sample), both times the vertical exaggeration.

Files per card `i` and tile `t`: `card-<i>-t<tt>-colour.webp` (lossy RGB albedo, lossless straight alpha) and `card-<i>-t<tt>-geometry.webp` (lossless: R, G = (n + 1) / 2 for the normal's along and across components, B = log-distance normalised between `encoding.log_d_min` and `log_d_max`; wall pixels write R = G = 255, a code outside the unit disc that no real normal reaches), each 204 columns wide. The manifest lists the cards nearest first (`near_m`, `far_m`: the median seam depths over the ring) and `tiles[t][i]` = `{ top, h, distance_m, colour, geometry }` or `null`; the runtime paints the cards last to first. The `bake_id` is the sha256 of the files, tile-major, then card, colour then geometry.

Budgets (`check_budget`; a cap is never raised to fit a bake, the lever is `TILES` = 64): every file of one tile at most 235 kB (1.5 MB per 72 degrees), every file of the place at most 7.5 MB, at most 6 cards, and the lit canvases (4 bytes x 202 columns x band rows, per card) of the worst run of N consecutive tiles, cyclic, at most 20 MB, where N = ceil(arc_max / 11.25) + 5 and arc_max = `HFOV_MAX_DEG` / s_min + 2 (r_max / Rmin) 57.3 degrees with Rmin the nearest card's smallest tile distance and s_min = Rmin / (Rmin + r_max). The loader decodes one tile at a time, so the decode transient is not gated.
