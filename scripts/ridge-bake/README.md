# ridge-bake

Offline baker for the ridge backdrop: it turns a Copernicus DEM into K+1 G-buffer cards per place, split by K authored seams and cut into 32 azimuth tiles round the whole 360 degree ring, plus a `manifest.json` (schema 4) and an `index.json` that the runtime reads. Each card tile is a surface WebP (the terrain's height, the wall flag and coverage) and a geometry WebP (normal and log-distance); no colour is baked: the manifest carries the place's authored material and the client shades the height with it. The baker is not shipped; `public/ridge/` holds its output. The client owns lighting, haze, colour and ink: no sun, haze, shadow or albedo is baked.

## Run

```sh
python3 -I scripts/ridge-bake/export.py <place-id> --tiles DIR --out public/ridge/ [--work DIR] [--sheet contact.png] [--switch-index]
python3 -I scripts/ridge-bake/export.py <place-id> --tiles DIR --vs-raw --sheet SHEETDIR [--work DIR] [--old public/ridge]
```

Needs Python 3 with `numpy`, `tifffile` and `imagecodecs`. `--tiles` is a directory of Copernicus GLO-30 tiles (never committed); `--work` caches the mosaic and rays (default `$TMPDIR/ridge-bake-work`); `--switch-index` points `out/index.json` at the new bake and deletes the previous one (without it the index and the old bake are left alone: the runtime keeps serving what it served); `--vs-raw` writes the operator sheets of a place into SHEETDIR and nothing under `--out` (see Terrain surface); `--sheet` writes the contact sheet at 1/4 of the ring's width (the ring composite, every card's strip alone and the depth image with every seam, the 32 tile boundaries drawn, lit by one fixed light). The bake prints `r_max_m`, the resident run and the worst fit error per card. `python3 -I scripts/ridge-bake/export.py --write-schema` rewrites `ridge-bake.schema.json`, which the tests pin.

Tests: `RIDGE_PY=<python> RIDGE_TILES=DIR scripts/ridge-bake/tests/run.sh [test_ridge.py|mutate.py]` (`mutate.py` re-introduces each rule's failure into a copy of the baker and fails if a test does not notice).

## Where the bakes ran

Yggdrasil (16 cores, 62 GB), as `mind-void-self`, work under `/var/tmp/ridge-bake` (never `/tmp`, a 31 GB tmpfs): `venv/` (python 3.13 with `numpy`, `tifffile`, `imagecodecs` from PyPI), `tiles/`, `work/<place>/`, one `out/<place>/` per place until its look is accepted. Tiles come from the public Copernicus DEM GLO-30 AWS Open Data bucket: `https://copernicus-dem-30m.s3.amazonaws.com/<name>/<name>.tif` with `<name>` = `Copernicus_DSM_COG_10_<N|S>dd_00_<E|W>ddd_00_DEM` (17 tiles, 598 MB).

The authored splits sit in depth gaps found by scanning candidate lists (seam medians printed per list): `seams` flees a split that falls inside a continuous mass of visible terrain to the nearest gap, so a split inside the mass yields an empty card 0 (Matterhorn's visible terrain is one mass from 4.9 to 15 km; gaps at 19, 28 and 43 km). Place splits at the gap medians the scan reports.

## Cards

`places.json` gives each preset `card_split_km`, an ordered list of 1 to 5 seam depths in km, and optionally `prefilter_m`. A preset without `card_split_km` is refused, and so is a split that leaves a card empty (the message names the splits); K splits make K+1 cards, at most `MAX_CARDS` = 6 per place. New places get a seam probe look before their first bake.

1. **March.** `stage0` marches 6400 azimuth columns of rays over the terrain surface M' (Terrain surface, below) with Earth curvature and refraction (`ridge.elev`), every height read by `ridge.cubic` (Catmull-Rom over 16 posts: it passes through every post and its gradient is continuous, where a bilinear read creased the normals at every 30 m cell), cached by a key of its inputs (`rays.v2`, the kernel name, the surface's `mosaic_key`).
2. **Low-pass.** `prefilter` blurs the sampled heights with a Gaussian of `prefilter_m` metres along each ray and across rays at equal distance (sigma = `prefilter_m` / (distance x column width) columns), then recomputes the angles. Roofs go, hills stay.
3. **View.** `set_view` maps angle to row linearly: the highest skyline angle of the whole ring sits at 4% of the frame height, the lowest at 66%, and the horizon keeps `GROUND_ROWS_MIN` = 150 rows under it (the runtime's `groundRowsNeeded`: (1 - 0.85) x 600 / 0.6, so the plane reaches the bottom of every viewport at rest). The scale is the lesser of the two fits, clamped to 0.5x to 10x vertical exaggeration (rulings `ridge-ring-vertical-anchors`, `ridge-ground-rows-below-horizon`: every azimuth sits inside one frame; a place whose whole-ring relief leaves too few ground rows is flattened below 1x); a skyline that cannot hold the rows even at 0.5x is refused naming `sky_deg` and the floor. `export.validate_manifest` refuses a plane with fewer than 150 rows under the horizon and `manifest.ts` refuses it at load.
4. **Seams.** `coverage` counts the visible rows of the opaque view per log-distance bin and column. `seams` finds one seam per authored split per column by dynamic programming (seam carving, forward energy): it sits in a gap between depths, moving it costs 2 per bin plus the visible rows it sweeps (so it never hops across a ridge between two columns and splits it side by side), a seam inside visible terrain is cheap to cross-fade, the authored depth only breaks ties. Seam k stays 0.4 nat beyond seam k-1. The DP runs over the ring three times side by side and keeps the middle lap, so a seam is continuous across the wrap (column 6399 to 0) and a tile boundary is only a crop.
5. **Cards.** `cards` runs per tile, on that tile's columns and the matching normals (it is column-independent: a tile is a crop of per-column results, so no seam appears at a tile boundary). It makes card i from the first visible hit at or beyond seam i-1 (layered depth: a farther card keeps the terrain a nearer ridge hides), alpha 1 minus a smoothstep over seam i in log-distance (the cross-fade), crest rows with fractional coverage, distance, normal and height interpolated between samples by angle, cropped to the rows where alpha is not zero. The rows of card i > 0 below the angle of its first sample (hit and first sample of the card) are its **wall**: the extruded face of the ridge the card starts at, which keeps that sample's distance, wears the ground material at its height with the face up, and is flagged `wall`. Card 0 also draws the **near march** (ruling `ridge-near-ground-outline`): the place's own DEM from `NEAR_START_M` = 1 m to the near clip `NEAR_CLIP_M` = 500 m (`near_march`: float64 bilinear samples every max(1 m, 0.02 d) up to 10 m, about 170 per column, low-passed with the place's own `prefilter_m`), joined to the far march by `join_near` on the whole ring before the tile cut, its angles capped per column at the angle of the clip sample so the near ground draws inside the silhouette of the terrain at the clip (a near hill above that edge is cut along it). Its rows are ordinary terrain with their own normal and height, flagged `near` in memory; `cards` refuses a march that starts at the clip. The runtime reads them as ground at the clip distance (they decode at `log_d_min`) and lights them like any card pixel. The alpha-over composite of the cards equals the opaque view; `check_cover` refuses a tile that differs by more than 0.02.
6. **Tiles.** The ring is absolute compass, column 0 = north, column c at azimuth (c + 0.5) / 6400 x 360; `places.json`'s `az_deg` / `look_at` is only the rest azimuth (`view.look_az_deg`). Tile t owns columns [200 t, 200 t + 200) (11.25 degrees) and its files hold [200 t - 3, 200 t + 203): three columns of real neighbour data each side (`APRON` = 3; the client reads normals and ink a few texels either way; the client crops it), taken periodically by `ridge.ring_cols`. Per card and tile the file is cropped to the rows where that tile's 8-bit alpha is not zero (`top`, `h`); a card that holds nothing in a tile is `null` there (the client interpolates its distance from the neighbours); a card with no solid pixel in any tile is refused.
7. **Distance and the offset range.** The bake holds no camera (ruling `ridge-camera-full-ring`). `pack` gives each card tile `distance_m` = exp(median ln d) over the tile's solid pixels that are neither wall nor near ground (its interior 200 columns); the client derives each card's slide from that and the viewer's offset r and heading. `camera.r_max_m` is the largest |r| (never above half the nearest terrain's distance) at which, for every tile, card and phi (24 steps of the camera's angle on the circle against its heading; the offset-only rotation mode moves phi round the whole circle), the least-squares line `a + s beta` fitted to the exact view angle `atan2(R sin beta - r sin phi, R cos beta - r cos phi)` over the widest viewport (`HFOV_MAX_DEG` = 72 / 1.1) errs by at most `FIT_PX_MAX` = 24 card px (the continuous error: the line by quadrature over the viewport, the worst error looked for on 513 points, so a handful of samples cannot undercount it); the distance d is that of the NEAREST solid (alpha >= 0.5, neither wall nor near ground) pixel the card draws in the tile's interior columns, which is where the slide errs most (the line is fitted for the tile's `distance_m`, the median, but the bound is held for the nearest terrain: a tile whose terrain spans 600 to 2000 m is bound at 600 m); each tile's near pixels (under the clip) count as terrain at the clip, 500 m, alongside its solid pixels, whatever else the tile holds (they are drawn at the clip; a tile of near ground only is slid there too), so a tile with any near pixel binds at 500 m at the latest; and the resident gate (below) must hold at r_max_m too. `camera.r_max_forward_m` is the same search at phi = 0 alone (same `FIT_PX_MAX`, the same half-distance cap, the same resident gate): the camera pushed forward along its heading, which is where the offset stays when the view direction turns with it (the modes 'still' and 'both'), so it is at least `r_max_m`; the client takes `r_max_m` for the offset-only mode and for a backward offset, `r_max_forward_m` for a forward one in the other modes (`view.ts` `offsetRange`). `FIT_PX_MAX` is provisional, tuned by eye (ruling `ridge-offset-range-fit`). A bake whose `r_max_m` is below `R_MAX_FLOOR_M` = 25 m is refused, naming the card and tile. `distance_m` and the nearest-terrain table exclude the near ground (a tile with nothing outside it gets `distance_m` = 500 m), so card 0's distances are never nearer than the near clip (`write_bake` refuses one that is), and `r_max_m` and `r_max_forward_m` hold for the nearest solid terrain each card draws in each tile, at or beyond 500 m, a tile with near pixels counts them at 500 m: the ground nearer than that rides card 0's slide and is not measured beyond that.
8. **Normals.** `normals` takes the along-ray slope and the across-ray slope at equal distance (neighbouring columns, same sample), both times the vertical exaggeration.

Files per card `i` and tile `t`: `card-<i>-t<tt>-surface.webp` (lossless RGBA: R, G = the height of the terrain surface, 16 bit with R high, between `encoding.h_min_m` and `h_max_m` (the lowest and highest height on any card pixel, rounded out to 0.1 m); B = 255 on wall pixels, else 0; A = straight coverage) and `card-<i>-t<tt>-geometry.webp` (lossless: R, G = (n + 1) / 2 for the normal's along and across components, a wall face up; B = log-distance normalised between `encoding.log_d_min` and `log_d_max`; `encoding.normal` = `rt8-up-walls`), each 206 columns wide. The manifest lists the cards nearest first (`near_m`, `far_m`: the median seam depths over the ring) and `tiles[t][i]` = `{ top, h, distance_m, surface, geometry }` or `null`; the runtime paints the cards last to first. The manifest also carries `material` (the place's material of `places.json`, validated by `ridge.check_material`) and `relief` (the canonical knobs the terrain was generalised with, or null; provenance, the runtime never reads it). The `bake_id` is the first 12 hex of the sha256 of the manifest text (id blanked) then the files, tile-major, then card, surface then geometry: a material or relief change is a new id and a new directory.

Budgets (`check_budget`; a cap is never raised to fit a bake, the lever is `TILES` = 64): every file of one tile at most 235 kB (1.5 MB per 72 degrees), every file of the place at most 7.5 MB, at most 6 cards, and the lit canvases (4 bytes x 202 columns x band rows, per card) of the worst run of N consecutive tiles, cyclic, at most 20 MB, where N = ceil(arc_max / 11.25) + 5 and arc_max = `HFOV_MAX_DEG` / s_min + 2 (r_max / Rmin) 57.3 degrees with Rmin the nearest card's smallest tile distance and s_min = Rmin / (Rmin + r_max). The loader decodes one tile at a time, so the decode transient is not gated.

## Terrain surface

The baker reads the survey through one function, `ridge.relief` (ruling `ridge-terrain-curvature-generalisation`), and every later stage reads only its result M' (`ridge.mosaic`, cached as `relief-<id>-<mosaic_key>.npy`; `raw_mosaic` is the GLO-30 tiles as they are, read by `relief` and by the eye-ground report alone). It follows the published method of Leonowicz, Jenny and Hurni, Cartographica 45(1) 64-74 (2010) and Cartographic Perspectives 67, 51-60 (2010), in our own numpy: a base surface B (Gaussian low-pass), a ridge weight and a valley weight from the Evans-Young curvature of low-passed copies (maximum curvature plus plan curvature for crests, the bend of a trough for valleys), a flat mask from slope, then `out = ws (B (1 + ridge_gain wr)) + (1 - ws) (B (1 - valley_gain wv))`, so crests are lifted and sharpened where the ground is steep and valleys deepened where it is flat. The factors act on absolute elevation, as published: sea level is the one fixed height. Curvature and slope use the true post spacing (21 m east-west against 31 m north-south at 47 N), and the Gaussians are round on the ground. `max_shift_m` bounds every post to less than that from the survey by `M + s tanh((out - M) / s)` (smooth, monotone, no clipped plateau).

`places.json` gives a preset an optional `relief` object of knobs in the published tool's units (all look knobs; the `_doc` string documents each; unknown keys are refused; detail 0 with both gains 0 is the identity and leaves the survey untouched). The shipped defaults are its 'large scale' preset (the finest grids, GLO-30's 30 m, need the weakest settings) with `detail` 3 and `max_shift_m` null (unbounded, ruling `ridge-silhouette-drift-unbounded-for-now`; set a bound to pin the skyline). `mosaic_key` = hash(raw tiles, canonical knobs, `RELIEF_VERSION`), so a changed knob is a new surface, new rays and (through the manifest) a new bake id. The eye stands on `cubic(M')` at the observer (`ground_override_m` still wins); the baker prints the survey's ground beside it and refuses a bake whose difference exceeds `max_shift_m` or whose near march stands above the eye within 5 m.

`export.py --vs-raw --sheet DIR` writes the operator sheets of a place: the old schema 3 bake in `public/ridge`, the preset's knobs and candidate B (`CAND_B`, the published 'medium' scale) at 1/4 of the ring's width stacked in that order, 4x crops of a Matterhorn crest and of the Grand Canyon's card 0 near ground, and a table of the skyline drift against the same preset with its relief removed (metres of crest height and frame rows, max and p99), the eye ground, relief seconds and peak RSS, the surface bytes against the old colour bytes and the worst tile against `TILE_BYTES_MAX`. The sheet evaluates the material over the heights (`ridge.albedo`); no colour is shipped.

## Materials

`places.json` `materials` is the one owner of ground colour (ruling `ridge-place-colour-authored`: the topology is real, the colour is authored). A material is `{ base: rgb, ramps: [{ from_m, to_m, colour }], steep: { colour } | null, snow: { from_m, to_m, colour } | null }`: `ridge.albedo(h, n_up, material)` lerps `base` through each ramp in order by height, then to the steep colour on steep faces (null: strata hold on cliffs), then to the snow colour on flat ground above its band. A place names its `material` (absent: `alpine`; an unknown name is refused, `ridge.load_material`). The baker copies it into the manifest (`material`) and ships no colour; `ridge.albedo` evaluates it for the contact sheets only. A material change reaches the bake id through the manifest text. `canyon` (Grand Canyon strata by height at the Unkar reach) was approved by eye on the contact sheet (ruling `grandcanyon-palette-approved`).
